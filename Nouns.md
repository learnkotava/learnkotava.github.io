## x. Nouns [$yolt$]

### x.x. No gender

Some languages have 'gender', where words need a different form of 'the' or adjectives that describe them have to match, e.g. Spanish has 2, German has 3.  

Kotava doesn't!  All Kotava nouns are of 'indeterminate' gender.

There is an ending you can add to nouns when you need to, or it's important to, express natural sex of an animal or a human being: $-ya$ for females, and $-ye$ for males.

### x.x. Euphonic agreement [$mamanyara$]

In English, we would never say '*a* apple' or '*an* newspaper'.  Even though *a* and *an* mean the same thing, there's only one right choice -- because it sounds better, i.e. it's 'euphonic'.

Kotava has a feature like this, too.

But whereas English bases the choice of *a* or *an* on the *first* letter of the word it modifies, for nouns, Kotava bases it on the **last** letter of the noun it modifies.  

You can almost think of it as making them rhyme.

Basically, if the noun you're talking about ends in a vowel, you will *add that vowel* to the word modifying it (i.e., adjectives, determiners, numerals, participles, possessive pronouns, as well as suffixes).

| Modified noun ends in  | Letter added to modifier for euphony |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| consonant, $w$, or $y$ | None                                 |
| $-a$                   | $-a$                                 |
| $-e$                   | $-e$                                 |
| $-i$                   | $-i$                                 |
| $-o$                   | $-o$                                 |
| $-u$                   | $-u$                                 |

Examples:

| Modifying word +       | Noun =                             | Kotava                                       | Note                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| $listaf$<br/>beautiful | $patectoy$<br/>landscape           | $listaf\ patectoy$<br/>a beautiful landscape | Nothing added to $listaf$, because $patectoy$ ends in $-y$ |
| $baroy$<br/>3          | $blucte$<br/><br/>item of clothing | $baroye\ blucte$<br/>3 pieces of clothing    | $blucte$ ends in $-e$, so $baroy$ adds an $-e$             |
| $bat$<br/>this         | $zveri$<br/>bird                   | $bati\ zveri$<br/>this bird                  | $zveri$ ends in $-i$, so $bat$ adds an $-e$                |
