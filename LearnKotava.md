# Sounds

Kotava is easy for English speakers to pronounce!

## Alphabet

The alphabet is the same as English, only without **h** and **q**:

        **a, b, c, d, e, f, g, i, j, k, l, m, n, o, p, r, s, t, u, v, w, x, y, z**

### c

The Kotava letter **c** sounds like 'sh' as in '**sh**oe'.

Click for an audio sample: [c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_postalveolar_fricative).

### j

The Kotava letter **j** is pronounced like the 's' in 'trea**s**ure', the 'z' in 'a**z**ure', or the 'j' in 'Ta**j** Mahal'.

Click for an audio sample: [j](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_postalveolar_fricative).

### r

The Kotava letter **r** is normally rolled or trilled, like in Italian or Spanish, but if that's hard for you, the *r* sound you're familiar with will do just fine. (Some of the French speakers can sound more like a French *r*).

Click for an audio sample: [r](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_dental_and_alveolar_trills#Voiced_alveolar_trill).

### x

Maybe the only unfamiliar sound is **x**, which is like a *j* in Spanish *<u>J</u>ose*, sometimes spelled like 'kh'. Or the German name *Ba<u>ch</u>* or a Scottish lo<u>ch</u>.

Click for an audio sample: [x](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_velar_fricative).

### Vowels

**a, e, i, o** and **u** are the vowels, and they sound the way they do in Spanish or Italian.

If you want to be precise, click the Wikipedia link and play the audio sample to the exact sound:

| Vowel | English equivalent      | Audio sample (Wikipedia)                                     |
| ----- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **a** | ah, as in f<u>a</u>ther | [a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_front_unrounded_vowel) |
| **e** | e, as in k<u>e</u>pt    | [e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-mid_front_unrounded_vowel) |
| **i** | ee, as in s<u>ee</u>    | [i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_front_unrounded_vowel) |
| **o** | oh, as in t<u>o</u>tal  | [o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-mid_back_rounded_vowel) |
| **u** | oo, as in t<u>oo</u>    | [u](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_back_rounded_vowel)  |

If you mix a vowel with **y**, it keeps its normal sound and just adds an 'ee' sound, e.g. **ay** sounds like *g<u>uy</u>* (not *d<u>ay</u>*).

#### Vowels with accent (´)

As you'll learn [later](#verbs), sometimes a Kotava word ends in a vowel with an accent mark on it, like **estú**. All this accent mark means is that you stress that vowel.

But you can expect to see: **á**, **é**, **í**, and **ú**. (Not *ó*)

> It isn't required to write the accent mark, if typing accents is hard for you.

#### Consonants

Some consonants we normally say with air added in, like *p* or *t*, are pronounced without that air in Kotava:

| Consonant | Audio sample (Wikipedia)                                     |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **k**     | [k](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_velar_plosive)   |
| **p**     | [p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_bilabial_plosive) |
| **t**     | [t](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_dental_and_alveolar_plosives) <br/>(*the top audio sample*) |

### w

In Kotava, **w** sounds just like English *w*. It never goes at the end of a word (unless it's a proper name).

### y, -ny

In Kotava, **y** never takes the place of a vowel like in English *cr<u>y</u>stal* or *m<u>y</u>riad*.

When you would otherwise have two vowels in a row, you have to add a **-y-**, like in the word **estursuyur** (he ate too much). There's a special suffix we'll talk about later 🚧, **-any**, where

The combination **ny** is pronounced close to *o<u>ni</u>on* or Spanish *a<u>ñ</u>o*:

| Letter | Audio sample (Wikipedia)                          |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **ny** | [ny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatal_nasal) |

### h (defunct)

> (You might see **h** in old Kotava texts from before 2011, because it used to exist before then. Don't worry about it -- it doesn't exist anymore.)

## Stress

Where you stress a word can make it mean different things in English (c<u>o</u>ntract = a thing vs. contr<u>a</u>ct = an action), and sometimes it's hard to know where in other languages.

But in Kotava there are 3 simple rules for word stress, based on the last letter of the Kotava word:

| Last Letter                                | Where to Stress       | Examples                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| -Vowel                                     | Next-to-last syllable | **blucte** <br/>(*BLOO-shteh*)<br/><br/>**kando** <br/>(*KAHN-doh*)<br/><br/>**feralia** <br/>(*feh-rah-LEE-ah*) |
| -Consonant, **-w**, or **-y**              | Last syllable         | **tuvel** <br/>(*too-VEHL*)<br/><br/>**felay**<br/> (*feh-LIE*)<br/><br/>**sayakik** <br/>(*sah-yah-KEEK*) |
| -[Accented vowel](#vowels-with-accent-(´)) | Accented vowel        | **estú** <br/> (eh-STOO) <br/> <br/>**lusteyé **<br/>(loo-steh-YEH) |

# Verbs

Verbs are a big part of Kotava, and you can do a lot with them!

We'll go one step at a time.

## Stem

In a Kotava dictionary, you will find verbs in their present tense form for "I do":

> **danká** - I sing
>
> **estú** - I eat

Without the accent mark, this is the stem, or base form, you'll be building on:

>  **DANKA** (to sing (stem))
>
>  **VEFA** (to play (stem))
>
>  **STUTE** (to dance (stem))
>
>  **ASKI** (to do (stem))
>
>  **PLEKU** (to put (stem))
>
>  **ESTU** (to eat (stem))

## Person endings: -, -l, -r, -t, -v, -c, -d

Unlike some languages with complex endings on verbs to show 'who' is doing the action, Kotava only uses a single letter to each person for you to remember:

| Person              | Ending               | Example                                      |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| I                   | accented final vowel | **danká**<br/>I sing                         |
| you (singular)      | **-l**               | **dankal**<br/>you (singular) sing           |
| he<br/>she<br/>it   | **-r**               | **dankar**<br/>he, she, it sings             |
| we (including you)  | **-t**               | **dankat**<br/>we sing, including you        |
| we (and not you)    | **-v**               | **dankav**<br/>we sing, but not you          |
| you (plural), y'all | **-c**               | **dankac**<br/>you (plural) sing, y'all sing |
| they                | **-d**               | **dankad**<br/>they sing                     |

In English, *we* and *us* are not always clear (do you mean, me too?).  In Kotava, it is easier to say this clearly: **-t** or **-v**.

You might have noticed the stress is always on the last syllable, following the stress rules (**1.13**).  That's the reason for the accent mark (**1.12**)!

## tí

A special and very common verb is **tí**, (stem: **TI**) which means 'to be'. Unlike other languages, in Kotava it follows the regular rules:

|         |                       |
| ------- | --------------------- |
| **tí**  | I am                  |
| **til** | you (singular) are    |
| **tir** | he, she, it is        |
| **tit** | we are, including you |
| **tiv** | we are, but not you   |
| **tic** | you (plural) are      |
| **tid** | they are              |

## Verb placement

As you'll see, in Kotava, verbs almost always go at the *end* of the sentence.

* **Slikviele *dankar*.** 
  * She *sings* all the time.

**Tí** is the only verb that doesn't.  Since it acts like an equal sign (=), you can think of it as needing to go in between the part of the sentence that *is* something else.

* **Inya *tir* listafa.** 
  * She *is* pretty.

* **Sin *tid* gruf.** 
  * They *are* intelligent.

# Nouns

## No gender

Some languages have 'gender', where words need a different form of 'the' or adjectives that describe them have to match, e.g. Spanish has 2, German has 3.

Kotava doesn't! All Kotava nouns are of 'indeterminate' gender.

### -ya, -ye

There is an ending you can add to nouns when you need to, or it's important to, express natural sex of an animal or a human being: **-ya** for females, and **-ye** for males.

## Euphonic agreement

In English, we would never say '*a* apple' or '*an* newspaper'. Even though *a* and *an* mean the same thing, there's only one right choice -- because it sounds better, i.e. it's 'euphonic'.

Kotava has a feature like this, too.

But whereas English bases the choice of *a* or *an* on the *first* letter of the word it modifies, for nouns, Kotava bases it on the **last** letter of the noun it modifies.

You can almost think of it as making them rhyme.

Basically, if the noun you're talking about ends in a vowel, you will *add that vowel* to the word modifying it (i.e., adjectives, determiners, numerals, participles, possessive pronouns, as well as suffixes).

| Modified noun ends in      | Letter added to modifier for euphony |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| consonant, **w**, or **y** | None                                 |
| **-a**                     | **-a**                               |
| **-e**                     | **-e**                               |
| **-i**                     | **-i**                               |
| **-o**                     | **-o**                               |
| **-u**                     | **-u**                               |

Examples:

​	**listaf** (*beautiful*) + **patectoy** (*landscape*) ➡️ **listaf patectoy** (*a beautiful landscape*)

​		Nothing is added to **listaf**, because **patectoy** ends in **-y**.

​	**baroy** (*3*) + **blucte** (*item of clothing*) ➡️ **baroy<u>e</u> blucte** (*3 items of clothing*)

​		Since **blucte** ends in **-e**, **baroy** adds an **-e**.

​	**bat** (*this*) + **zveri** (*bird*) ➡️ **bat<u>i</u> zveri** (*this bird*)

​		Since **zveri** ends in **-i**, **bat** adds an **-e**.

# Plurals

Plurals are not marked in Kotava directly like English -*s* or -*es*, for example.

Instead, we use other ways to imply the noun is plural.

For instance, with 5 + thing, it's clear that there are multiple of that thing.  In this way, we say that the number 5 is "semantically plural":

* **Koe kelt va aluboy bitej wí.**
  * *[in] [sky] [object>] [five] [star] [I-see]*	
  * I see five stars in the sky.

The word for star, **bitej**, is not plural by itself, but it doesn't have to be.  So we can say that the word **bitej** alone in fact can mean *star* or *stars*.

We can also see a noun is plural when the verb used with it is plural, like:

* **Koe kelt jontik bitej jebed.**
  * *[in] [sky] [many] [star] [they-shine]*
  * Many stars shine in the sky.

## yon

We can also use the Kotava word **yon**, meaning 'some' or 'a certain number of', to denote plural.

* **Koe kelt va yon bitej rowí.**

  - *[in] [sky] [object>] [some] [star] [can-see]*

  - I can see some stars in the sky.


## yo, se (defunct)

> *In the old version of the language, two words could mark plural by going after the noun they modify, both interchangeable: **se** and **yo**.  They don't exist anymore, but you might see them in really old Kotava material from before 2008.*

# Past: -y-

Kotava forms the past by inserting a simple suffix before the personal ending (**2.2**) of the verb: the letter **y-**, plus the vowel right before the **y**.  

In other words, add -**y** + the vowel at the end of the stem (**2.1**):

| Verb Stem           | Present Tense        | Past Tense             |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **danka** - to sing | **danká** - I sing   | **dankayá** - I sang   |
| **dole** - to sell  | **doler** - he sells | **doleyer** - he sold  |
| **ti** - to be      | **tir** - she is     | **tiyir** - she was    |
| **estu** - to eat   | **estud** - they eat | **estuyud** - they ate |

That's it!

---

#  Future: -t-

Kotava forms the future a lot like the [past](Past.md), only with -**t**-.  That is, the future is formed by inserting a simple suffix before the personal ending (**2.2**) of the verb: the letter **t-**, plus the vowel right before the **t**.

In other words, add -**t** + the vowel at the end of the stem (**2.1**):

| Verb Stem           | Present Tense        | Past Tense                  |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **danka** - to sing | **danká** - I sing   | **dankatá** - I will sing   |
| **dole** - to sell  | **doler** - he sells | **doleter** - he will sell  |
| **ti** - to be      | **tir** - she is     | **titir** - she will be     |
| **estu** - to eat   | **estud** - they eat | **estutud** - they will eat |

That's it!

# Negative

## me (not)

In Kotava, the word for '*not*' is **me**.  It is placed right before the verb you are negating.

​	**Me estú**.

​	*[not] [I-eat]*

​	I am not eating.

​	**Koe Paris me irubá.**

​	*[in] [Paris] [not] [I-live]*

​	I do not live in Paris.

​	**Va in me kiaval.**

​	*[object >] [he] [not] [you-greet]*

​	You are not greeting him.

## men (not yet)

To say "not yet," use **men**, also right before the verb, in the same place as **me**:

​	**Koe Paris men irubá.**

​	*[in] [Paris] [not-yet] [I-live]*

​	I do not live in Paris yet.

​	**Sin va sint men kiavad.**

​	*[they] [object >] [each-other] [not-yet] [they-greet]*

​	They are not greeting each other yet. / They are not yet greeting each other.

## mea (not anymore)

To say "not anymore," use **mea**, again right before the verb:

​	**Koe Paris mea irubá.**

​	*[in] [Paris] [not-anymore] [I live]*

​	I don't live in Paris anymore.

​	**Sin va sint mea kiavad.**

​	*[they] [object >] [each-other] [not-anymore] [they-greet]*

​	They're not greeting each other anymore.

​	**Noelon, viele va int tuidulá, bam kan Kotava mea otá kire va Solresol ava noelton toz raveyé.**

​	*[currently] [when] [object >] [oneself] [I-heat-up], [then] [by-means-of] [Kotava] [not-anymore] [count] [because] [object >] [Solresol] [language] [recently] [start-to] [I-learned].

​	*(context: counting to ten during exercise)* Currently, when I warm up, I no longer rely on the Kotava language because I recently started learning the Solresol language.  ([Tutuzara](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p2C5lZOUL1k&t=63) ~ 2018)

## vol (not at all)

Kotava also has a useful word, **vol**, for the idea that not only does the verb not happen, *all is done for it not to happen.*  It is best translated as "not at all," but Kotava materials also translate it as "on the contrary," "in contrast to," or "surely not".  Look at these examples, and you'll get the idea.

​	**Vol estú.**

​	*[contrary] [I-eat]*

​	I <u>do my best not to</u> eat.

​	**Sin va sint vol kiavad.**

​	*[they] [object >] [each-other] [contrary] [they-greet]*

​	They <u>do all it takes to not</u> greet each other.

​	**Xum, dile vol!** 

​	*[hmm] [sometimes] [contrary]*

​	Hm! It's sometimes <u>the opposite</u>.  ([Va Mona Anamlaní](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EwMjcQIZbFg&t=240) ~ 2017)

---

​	**Paelpa toz aultover neken vol idular.  Adim vol idulayar vomion.**

​	*[sweating] [start-to] [it-abounds] [nonetheless] [contrary] [it-is-hot].  [finally] [contrary] [it-was-hot] [before]*

​	(*colloquially, context is after jumping up and down outside*): 

​	The sweating really starts, yet it's <u>the furthest thing from</u> hot out.  --At least, it <u>was the furthest thing from</u> hot out before.[^1]

---

**Ombamukot tir rodjesira va kevelik, i debala tode gazikye zo rogralomer ise vol rototcer.**

*[game+objective] [it-is] [cause-the-failure] [object>] [adversary], [< substitute] [situation] [in-which-situation] [king-male] [passive >] [it-can-capture] [and] [contrary] [it-can-escape]*

*(context: chess)* objective of the game is to [checkmate] your opponent, that being the situation where the king is able to get captured and <u>has no way to escape</u>.

---

# Objects

🚧

## Direct objects: **va**

A very common word in Kotava is **va**.

**Va** is used to mark the noun following it as the object of the action, i.e. the noun the action is directly happening to or is being directly acted upon.  The noun following **va** is called the 'object complement.'

​	**Va sumewisiki wir.** He's looking at the television.

​	**Va in kiaval.** You're greeting him.

## Transitive vs. intransitive

A verb (or other word acting as a verb, like certain [participles](#participles) that does an action directly on something using **va** is called '<u>transitive</u>'. In English, examples are: to bind, to rip, to nourish, to unwrap. The opposite, an action that doesn't take an object directly like that, is called '<u>intransitive</u>'. Some English examples are: to sit, to ripen, to stay. Depending on the context, some English verbs can be either transitive or intransitive, e.g. to escape (I escaped. I escaped the house.), to sing (I was singing. I sang a song.), to see (I can see! Did you see me?), or to write (I like to write. I am writing a book.)

Verbs are not marked as transitive in the dictionary, but you can get a sense from the meaning, other words from the same root ([Kotapedia](https://kotava.org/kotapedia) is great for this) and also from reading in Kotava which ones are used that way.

### Making a verb transitive: **-si**

Starting with a verb that doesn't take a direct object (an 'intransitive' verb), you can make it transitive by adding the suffix **-si** to the stem:

​	**KENIBE** (to sleep) ➡️ **KENIBESI** (to put sb/sth to sleep)

​	**LUBE** (to fall) ➡️ **LUBESI** (to fell sth, to make sb/sth fall)

​	**AELE** (to hunger) ➡️ **AELESI** (to starve sb/sth)

Since **-si** is added to the *stem*, the personal endings are added on to it:

​	**WIDLA** (to shake, to move (int.))

​	➡️ **WIDLASI** (to shake sb/sth, to move sb/sth)

* **... nume ine [^2] skotcapar eke va mona *widlasir*.**..

  * *[so] [she] [she-trembles-big] [to-such-an-extent] [obj >] [mona] [she-shakes-tr.]*

  * ... and she trembled till she shook the house... (*[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/11/11-h/11-h.htm#chap04)*)

---

### Making a verb intransitive: **-we**

Starting with a transitive verb -- one that takes an object -- you can make it intransitive by adding **-we** to the stem.

​	**BETA** (to change sth) ➡️ **BETAWE** (to change)

​	**TUEBELTA** (to blacken sth) ➡️ **TUEBELTAWE** (to blacken, to turn black)

​	**ESTU** (to eat) ➡️ **ESTUWE** (to feed oneself)[^4]

​	**FENKU** (to open sth) ➡️ **FENKUWE** (to open, on its own)

​	**BUDE** (to close/shut sth) ➡️ **BUDEWE** (to close/shut, on its own)

Again, it is added to the *stem*:

* **Tuvel paedanaf gu kusafa bayna stasa van burmotaxo ve *fenkuwer* aze ton sepuna gla *budewer*.**
  * The green baize door that led to the kitchen regions *swung open* and *shut* with a muffled thud. (*[The Garden Party](https://www.scribd.com/doc/206707725/Matelakapa-ke-Katherine-Mansfield?v=0.236#content=query:wer,pageNum:7,indexOnPage:0,bestMatch:false)*)

---

### Ditransitive: **gu**

A verb that is transitive but that takes *two* objects is called 'ditransitive'. In English, we think of these as verbs that take a 'direct object' and an 'indirect object'[^3], e.g. to give, to thank for, to take off of. There aren't many of these.

> <u>Note:</u> They don't *have* to take both objects in a sentence. They can have just a direct object with **va**.

The direct object is introduced by **va** like normal, and the indirect object is marked by the word **gu**:

**zilí** (to give): The thing being given gets **va**, and the person it's being given to gets **gu**:

* **«Vexe toktan va waks fu zilir ?» sin tamon erud.** 
  * “But who is [going] to give the prizes?” quite a chorus of voices asked. (*[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland](https://www.europalingua.eu/bad_wikikrenteem/index.php?title=Stuveem_ke_Alice_koe_Zultaxo_Luz03)*)

**grewá** (to thank for): The person being thanked gets **va**, and what they're getting thanked for gets **gu**.

# Exclamations

## man, man-

Exclamations in Kotava must end in an exclamation mark, which takes a space before it.

You can make a simple sentence into an exclamation using the word **man**:

* **Gazik tiyir.**
  * He was the king.

* **Man gazik tiyir !** 
  * What a king he was!

You can also use **man-** as a prefix to construct words using the elements in ([x.x.1](RelativePronouns.md)), to make pronouns:

- **Mantan tir !** 
  - What a guy! He's quite a guy! He's some guy!
- **Va mancoba askil !** 
  - What a thing you're doing! That's quite a thing you're doing! Isn't that something (the thing you're doing)!

Or to make adverbs:

- **Maneke kobal !** 
  - You're working so much!
- **Manlize irubayá !**  
  - I lived in such a place! What a place it was I lived in!

# Affirmation

## en

Like the [negative particles](#negative), Kotava also has one for reinforcement, or being extra assertive, affirming or emphasizing the statement.

The affirmative is indicated by **en**.  **En** by itself means 'yes'.

- **En estú.** 
  - I really am eating.
- **Koe Paris en irubá.** 
  - I really do live in Paris. I *do* live in Paris.
- **Van in en kiaval.** 
  - You're greeting him straightforwardly. You're 'straight up' greeting him.

# Commands

## Imperative

Verbs can be used for commands -- also called an "imperative" or "injunctive".  These mood particles are used for orders or prohibitions, but also for wishes, desires, or prayers.

When using this verb form in Kotava, you must end the sentence with a double exclamation mark.

The other ways to modify verbs can also be used with imperatives.  Examples:

|                |                                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **KE**         | to wait                                           |
| **Ké !!**      | Wait(, me)! (said to oneself)                     |
| **Kel !!**     | Wait! (said to a single person)                   |
| **Ketet !!**   | We shall wait!                                    |
| **Me ker !!**  | Let him not wait! He is not to wait!              |
| **Zo ked !!**  | Let's wait on them!                               |
| **Toz kel !!** | Start waiting!                                    |
| **Rokec !!**   | You can wait! You shall find the ability to wait! |

### Injunction grades

Also called 'imperative intensives', these particles are only usable with an imperative / injunctive.

Kotava has 3 grades of intensity:

|             |                                                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **vay !?**  | Please<br />(*polite imperative, wishfulness, prayer*)       |
| **djay !!** | Go for it! I insist! I'm telling you!<br />(*insisting, simple command*) |
| **xay !!**  | That is an order!<br />(*absolute imperative, absolute order*) |

When it comes after the verb, these particles *repeat* the order, directly.

* **Estul vay !?**
  * Eat, please! Could you please eat!

- **Estul djay !!**
  - I insist you eat! Go ahead and eat! Come on, eat!

- **Estul xay !!**

  - I command you to eat! Eat, that's an order! EAT!


---

When placed before the verb, they are more like 'indirect address'. 

- **Vay ker !?**
  - If he'd wait!

- **Djay ker !!**

  - Let him wait! I wish he would wait!

- **Xay ker !!**

  - You WILL let him wait! Let him wait, that's an order!

# Proper Nouns

## Proper Nouns

A proper noun refers to the proper name of a person, place or thing (with a first capital letter).

## Classifiers

Kotava uses the type of thing referred to along with the name, similar to the common English construction 'the city of Greenville', 'the State of Georgia', 'Lake Michigan' or 'the painting *The Harvesters*'.  (English has no elegant way of distinguishing Georgia the country from the U.S. state -- with classifiers, it would.)  In these examples, the words 'city', 'state', 'lake' or 'painting' when paired with the proper name are called *classifiers*.

Classifiers aren't necessary in Kotava for extremely common proper nouns (like countries, big cities, known places, famous or well-known people, etc.):

- **Franca**
  - France

- **Espana**
  - Spain


But it wouldn't technically be incorrect to use one if desired, i.e. **Franca patecta**.

Most common nouns can be used as a proper noun classifier.  The most common ones used this way have to do with: 

* persons
  * **ayik** (human being), **guazik** (elderly person), **jotik** (youth), **korik** (person, individual), **rumeik** (little child), **velik** (child), **yik** (young person), and other words referring to individuals
    * **Johnson guazik**
      * Old Man Johnson
* kinship terms
  * **kosayik** (cousin), **ziavik** (uncle, aunt), and all other kinship words
* polite forms of addressing someone 
  * **eyik** (individual, citizen, sir), **weltik** (Mr./Mrs.), **jiomaik** (lord), **jadiwik** (general) and all other military grades
    * **Adams weltikya**
      * Mrs. Adams
    * **Frost jiomaik**
      * Lord Frost
* titles
  * **gazik** (king), **sersik** (prince), **taneagadesik** (prime minister)
    * **Herod gazik**
      * King Herod
    * **Charles sersik**
      * Prince Charles
    * **Blair taneagadesik**
      * Prime Minister Blair
* places
  * **bira** (sea), **bost** (big river), **kuksa** (river), **mefta** (mountain), **uzda** (lake), **wida** (village), **widava** (city), and all other geographical words
    * **Baikal uzda**
      * Lake Baikal
    * **Fuji mefta**
      * Mt. Fuji
    * **London wizdava** 
      * the city of London[^6]
* geographic/administrative entities
  * **patecta** (country), **gola** (region), **dota** (commune), **tadava** (continent), **winta** (planet), and all words for geographic or administrative entities
* names of species
  * **tamava** (world), **bliind** (kingdom (biol.)), **dansok** (phylum), **pula** (class), **veem** (order), **yasa** (family (biol.)), **grelt** (tribe), **oxi** (genus), **katca** (species), **apta** (subspecies) 
* works of art or media
  * **neva** (book), **luz** (chapter), **teliz** (article (of publication)), **fela** (journal), **gabot** (section), and other similar words
* groups, enterprises, companies
  * **lospa** (group), **veem** (order, corporation), **ja** (enterprise), **gesia** (association), **pako** (party), **sistu** (company) and other similar words
* measure or unit
  * **soluma** (distance), **lum** (dimension), **lumolk** (unit of measurement)
* currency
  * **tal** (currency)
    * **Euro tal**
      * the Euro
* language
  * **ava** (language)

# Pronouns

## Pronouns

There are different kinds of pronouns.  Personal pronouns, like *I*, *she*, or *them*; reflexive personal pronouns

We'll look at all of these here.

### Personal Pronouns

Just like English, Kotava has personal pronouns (I, she, they).  But unlike English, you normally don't need to use them, because the verb itself contains that information, e.g. **estú** means *I eat*.  'They are only needed if you have to be precise.  When the pronoun is the subject of the sentence (the one doing the verb), you use these:

|         |                |
| ------- | -------------- |
| **jin** | I              |
| **rin** | you (singular) |
| **in**  | he, she, it    |
| **min** | we (inclusive) |
| **cin** | we (exclusive) |
| **sin** | they           |
| **win** | you (plural)   |

Examples:

* **Min betlize kenibeyet.**
  - *We* (including you) *slept anywhere*.

- **Cin betlize kenibeyet.** 
  - *We* (not you) *slept anywhere*.

Because **in** can mean 'he', 'she,' or 'it', you may sometimes need to specify the gender of who you're talking about.  To do this, you add the male suffix **-ye** or female suffix **-ya** to the pronoun.  You can add these to any of the personal pronouns, actually, as needed:

​	**inye** (*he*, specifically)

​	**inya** (*she*, specifically)

​	**sinya** (*they*, meaning the females)

When you use a subject pronoun, you never repeat it in the same sentence:

* **(Rin) Va mona disukel nume kalil da listafa.**
  * You look at the house and (you) say it's pretty.

Here, you wouldn't need to use **rin** at all, since the verbs -- **disukel** and **kalil** -- already have the **-l** ending meaning 'you (singular)'.

### The former / The latter

Personal pronouns normally don't change their form.  But for situations where you refer to two different things called '*it*' or '*they*', you can use [euphony rules](#euphonic-agreement) to specify:

*  **Perake koe werd terigeyer, mewison va vamoetalasi zveripi.  *Ini* va *ine* ipeyer.** 
  * *[snake] [in] [grass] [it-crawled], [not-see-adv.] [object >] [above-fly-ing] [bird-big]. [it-i] [object >] [it-e] [it-pounced]*
  * The snake crawled in the grass, without seeing the big bird flying above.  The latter (the bird) pounced on the former (the snake).

Here, which 'it' you are referring to in the second sentence is marked by using the word's euphony letter from the first sentence -- i.e., In+i (to mean **zveripi**, the big bird) vs. in+e (to mean **perake**, the snake).

>  This rather rare method can only be used with **in** or **sin**.  It's rare because it is only useful when the two *it* or *they* nouns you're distinguishing from the prior sentence have *different euphony endings* -- if both words ended in **-i**, you couldn't distinguish them this way.

### Reflexive Pronoun: **int**

A reflexive pronoun refers to the subject doing some action to itself (*themselves, myself*...).

In Kotava, the reflexive pronoun is **int**.  It never takes a gender suffix, because it doesn't need to.  

* **In icde *int* fereon pulvir.**
  * *[he] [about] [self] [often] [he-talks]*
  * He often talks about himself.

This helps you be clearer when two people are involved:

* **In icde *in* fereon pulvir.**

  * *[he] [about] [him] [often] [he-talks]*

  * He often talks about him (someone else).

### Reciprocal Pronoun: **sint**

A reciprocal pronoun refers to *multiple* people doing some action to each other (*one another, each other*).

In Kotava, the reciprocal pronoun is **sint**.  It never takes a gender suffix either.  Naturally, it can only be used with plural subjects.

- **Sin va *sint* disuked.**
  - *[they] [object >] [each-other] [they-look-at]*
  - They look at each other. (1 is looking at 2, and 2 is looking at 1.)
- **Sin va *int* disuked.**
  - *[they] [object >] [self] [they-look-at]*
  - They each look at themselves. (1 is looking at himself, and 2 is looking at himself.)

- **Sin va sin disuked.**
  - *[they] [object >] [them] [they-look at]*
  - They look at them. (Multiple people are looking at other people.)


### Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns, like '*mine*' or '*theirs*' are for things referred to as belonging to someone.

In Kotava, they take the form of the determiner **tel** (meaning *the* or *the one*), the [personal pronoun](#personal-pronouns) + [adjective](#adjectives) ending **-af**:

|               |                  |
| ------------- | ---------------- |
| **tel jinaf** | mine             |
| **tel rinaf** | yours (singular) |
| **tel inaf**  | his, hers, its   |
| **tel minaf** | ours (inclusive) |
| **tel cinaf** | ours (exclusive) |
| **tel winaf** | yours (plural)   |
| **tel sinaf** | theirs           |

* **Tel sinaf tir lokaliaf dam jinaf.**

  * *[the-one] [they-adj.] [it-is] [fast-er] [than] [the-one] [I-adj.]*

  * His is faster than mine. 

There is also a reflexive version of this:

* **Va tel intaf estuyur.**

  * *[object >] [the-one] [self-adj.] [he-ate]*

  * He ate his own.

And there are two other forms we will cover later ([x.x](#determiners)). 🚧

* **tel dal intaf**

* **tel dil intaf**

# Relative Pronouns

You can do <u>a lot</u> in Kotava with these types of pronouns.  Luckily, it's all in a very logical system.  Take your time!

## Relative Pronouns 🚧

A relative pronoun, like 'who,' 'which', or 'that', lets you describe something using a verb nested within the larger sentence.  For example, 'I like dogs <u>*that* know tricks</u>' or 'This is the man <u>*who* called yesterday</u>'.

> <u>Note:</u> Kotava doesn't use these the way English does, instead opting for [participles](#participles) -- basically, instead of "the person who eats bread" the Kotava sentence looks more like "the person eating bread."

There are four basic compound relative pronouns:

|          |                                                              |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **coba** | *thing*, as a relative pronoun meaning *what, that which*    |
| **tan**  | *one*, as a relative pronoun meaning *one (unknown)*         |
| **tel**  | *the*, as a relative pronoun meaning *one (known)*, *the one, this one* |
| **tol**  | *two*, as a relative pronoun meaning *either*                |

## Pronoun Building

Kotava has a fun, easy-to-learn system for making these kinds of pronouns.  They are made out of pieces that become familiar quickly.  We will go through each of the pieces and their meanings when combined with the 4 basic relative pronouns above.

> The types of pronouns here include: demonstratives (like 'this' or 'that'), negative (like 'nobody' or 'nowhere'), indefinite (like 'somewhere', 'anybody', 'none', 'everyone' or 'most'), quantitative (like 'few', 'many', 'several ones' or 'too many') and interrogative (like 'which one' or 'which kind').
>
> Since the pieces do not change in strange ways like other languages, building new ways to express what you want in Kotava can feel more like a game than a chore.

Each of the pieces below are also Kotava words in their own right, like in English, e.g. 'anybody' and 'somebody', and 'nobody' are all made of pieces added to '-body' that are words on their own (any, some, no).

## 🚧

- **bata gola**
  - *this region*

# Compound Words

## Compound Words

Compound words in English are made of multiple words combined, such as seahorse, foxglove.  But in English, these words are somewhat fixed -- instead of making <u>new</u> compound words, you have to use multiple separate words: like *coffee mug*, *picture frame*, or *court case*.

In Kotava, you can be a lot more creative with forming compound words.  There are 4 main ways to do it.

---

### Noun + Noun ➡️ Compound Noun

You can combine two nouns together, if they have one of the following relationships:

* object / transitive (*va*, i.e. one is the object of the other)

  **iaxe** (factory)
  **tie** (gas)
  **iaxe *va* tie** (gas-fired power plant)
  *[manufacture-location] [object >] [gas]*
  	➡️ **tieiaxe** (gas-fired power plant)

* possession / ownership (*ke*, i.e. one possesses the other)

  **widava *ke* winka** (provincial town)
  *[town] [of] [province]*
  	➡️ **winkawidava** (provincial town)

* content / partitive (*dem*, i.e. one contains the other)

* destination (*tori*, i.e. one is used for another, its "destined use" is the other, think "for", like a vase for flowers or a cup for coffee)

  **bilaga *tori* fadxa** (cup for coffee)
  *[cup] [used-for] [coffee]*
  	➡️ **fadxabilaga** (coffee cup)

* purpose (*ta*, i.e. [])

  **bruxa *ta* sutera**
  *[feather] [goal >] [writing]*
  	➡️ **suterabruxa** (quill)

* matter (*kum*, i.e. one is made out of the other, one is the material of the other)

  **kudja *kum* inta**
  *[statue] [made-of] [wood]*
  	➡️ **intakudja **(wood statue)


Luckily, if you know the two nouns have one of these relationships, but can't precisely figure out which one, it is often easier to compound them anyway rather than trying to pinpoint the right preposition.

### Adjective + Noun ➡️ Compound Noun

You can add a noun and its adjective together, too, but only one attribute can be combined with its noun (not multiple).

Here's how:

1. Strip down adjective to stem by removing adjective ending: 
   *pulodafa ➡️  pulod-, bemafa ➡️ pemaf-*.

   * *Note:* With verbal adjectives {}, you won't strip it down first.

2. Add an **-a** if the noun starts in a consonant.

3. Add the result to the front of the noun:

   **pulod** (parliament)
   **vertuma** (system, regime)
   pulodafa vertuma
   *[parliament (adj.)] [system]*
   *➡️ pulod~~afa~~ vertuma*
   *➡️ pulod + **a** + vertuma*
   	➡️ **pulodavertuma** 'parliamentary system'

   **bema** (school)
   **neva** (book)
   bemafa neva
   *[school (adj.)] [book]*
   *➡️ bem~~afa~~ neva*
   *➡️ bem+ **a** + neva*
   	➡️ **bemaneva** 'schoolbook'

4. Only one adjective can be compounded this way:

   **warzafa** (new)

   ❌ ~~**warzabemaneva**~~
   ✅ **warzafa bemaneva**
   ✅ **warzafa bemafa neva**

> *Note:* Compounding like this is always optional, i.e. 'pulodafa vertuma' and 'bemafa neva' are allowed and mean the same thing.

### Determiner + Substantive (Time) ➡️ Adverb (Time)

Compound words are often to make adverbs of time that express a phrase about time using prepositions like **ba** (at, in), **bak** (during), or **remi** (while), e.g.:

* **ba kot bartiv**
  * every hour

Instead of the preposition, you compound the determiner and the time noun (removing euphonic endings {[x.x](Nouns.md)}, and removing -**a** from time nouns ending in **-a**), and add the adverb ending **-on** {}:

* **ba kot bartiv**

  * every hour

    *~~ba~~ kot + bartiv + **on***
    ➡️ **kotbartivon**

    * every hour (adv.)

- **ba kota gazda**
  - every morning
    ​*~~ba~~ kot~~a~~ + gazd~~a~~ + **on***
    ​➡️ **kotgazdon** 
    - every morning (adv.)
- **bak bete sare** 
  - from time immemorial, in any age
    ​*~~bak~~ bet~~e~~ + sare + **on***
    ​➡️ **betsareon**
    - from time immemorial (adv.)
- **ba bata vula**
  - in this instant
    ​*~~ba~~ bat~~a~~ + vula + **on***
    ​➡️ **batvulon**
    - this instant (adv.)
- **ba mek gemelt**
  - in no moment, at no point in time
    ​*~~ba~~ mek + gemelt + **on***
    ​➡️ **mekgemelton**
    - at no point in time (adv.)

Example:

* **Kotvielon is kotbartivon, kota gasiza is kot awalkik va bata tiguafa zobexa loloon sovibad ise tanda kalion rucad.**
  * *Every day and every hour* every shell and every death cuts into this thin support, and the years waste it rapidly. (*All Quiet on the Western Front*)

You can also combine this way for prepositional phrases using **mali** (since) and **kali** (until):

- **mali arintaf viel**
  - since yesterday
    *mali + arint~~af~~ + **on***
    ➡️ **maliarinton** 
    - since yesterday (adv.)

* **kali eldef viel**
  * until tomorrow
    ​*kali + eld~~ef~~ + **on***
    ​➡️ **kalieldeon** 
    * until tomorrow (adv.)

This compounding happens with nouns having to do with time, mainly:

* **afiz** (daytime), **aksat** (month), **bartiv** (hour), **gazda** (early morning), **gemelt** (moment), **kiel** (afternoon), **miafiz** (noon), **riel** (morning), **safta** (week), **sare** (age), **siel** (evening), **tanda** (year), **ugal** (time), **vanafiz** (dawn), **verast** (second), **viel** (day), **vula** (instant), **wexa** (minute).

It also applies to the **-cek** []🚧 forms of these nouns (duration or content), such as:

* **afizcek** (daytime), **gazdacek** (morning period), **kielcek** (afternoon period), **rielcek** (morning period), **sielcek** (evening period), **vielcek** (whole day).

### 'One-by-One' ➡️ Adverb

You can make an adverb to express progression, repetition, or iteration, in the sense of 'piece by piece' or 'one by one', by compounding the term with itself (removing **-a** from the first term) and again adding adverb ending -**on**:

* **bar** 
  * 3
    ​*bar + bar + **on**:*
    ​➡️ **barbaron** 
    * in threes, 3-by-3

* **abic** 
  * a little
    ​*abic + abic + **on**:*
    ​➡️ **abicabicon** 
    * little by little

* **tuvel**
  * door
    ​*tuvel + tuvel + **on**:*
    ​➡️ **tuveltuvelon** 
    * door to door (adv.)

Another example, from the Kotava official grammar:

* **kalikalion** 
  * making great strides[^8]
