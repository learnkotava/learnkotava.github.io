## x. Pronouns

There are different kinds of pronouns.  Personal pronouns, like *I*, *she*, or *them*; reflexive personal pronouns

We'll look at all of these here.

### x.1. Personal Pronouns

Just like English, Kotava has personal pronouns (I, she, they).  But unlike English, you normally don't need to use them, because the verb itself contains that information, e.g. **estú** means *I eat*.  'They are only needed if you have to be precise.  When the pronoun is the subject of the sentence (the one doing the verb), you use these:

|         |                |
| ------- | -------------- |
| **jin** | I              |
| **rin** | you (singular) |
| **in**  | he, she, it    |
| **min** | we (inclusive) |
| **cin** | we (exclusive) |
| **sin** | they           |
| **win** | you (plural)   |

Examples:

​	**Min betlize kenibeyet.** (*We* (including you) *slept anywhere*.)

​	**Cin betlize kenibeyet.** (*We* (not you) *slept anywhere*.)

Because **in** can mean 'he', 'she,' or 'it', you may sometimes need to specify the gender of who you're talking about.  To do this, you add the male suffix **-ye** or female suffix **-ya** to the pronoun.  You can add these to any of the personal pronouns, actually, as needed:

​	**inye** (*he*, specifically)

​	**inya** (*she*, specifically)

​	**sinya** (*they*, meaning the females)

When you use a subject pronoun, you never repeat it in the same sentence:

​	**(Rin) Va mona disukel nume kalil da listafa.** (*You look at the house and (you) say it's pretty.*)

Here, you wouldn't need to use **rin** at all, since the verbs -- **disukel** and **kalil** -- already have the **-l** ending meaning 'you (singular)'.

### x.1.1. The former / The latter

Personal pronouns normally don't change their form.  But for situations where you refer to two different things called '*it*' or '*they*', you can use euphony rules ([x.x](Nouns.md)) to specify:

 	**Perake koe werd terigeyer, mewison va vamoetalasi zveripi.  *Ini* va *ine* ipeyer.** 

​	*[snake] [in] [grass] [it-crawled], [not-see-adv.] [object >] [above-fly-ing] [bird-big]. [it-i] [object >] [it-e] [it-pounced]*

​	The snake crawled in the grass, without seeing the big bird flying above.  The latter (the bird) pounced on the former (the snake).

Here, which 'it' you are referring to in the second sentence is marked by using the word's euphony letter from the first sentence -- i.e., In+i (to mean **zveripi**, the big bird) vs. in+e (to mean **perake**, the snake).

This rather rare method can only be used with **in** or **sin**.  It's rare because it is only useful when the two *it* or *they* nouns you're distinguishing from the prior sentence have *different euphony endings* -- if both words ended in **-i**, you couldn't distinguish them this way.

### x.x. Reflexive Pronoun

A reflexive pronoun refers to the subject doing some action to itself (*themselves, myself*...).

In Kotava, the reflexive pronoun is **int**.  It never takes a gender suffix, because it doesn't need to.  

​	**In icde *int* fereon pulvir.**

​	*[he] [about] [self] [often] [he-talks]*

​	He often talks about himself.

This helps you be clearer when two people are involved:

​	**In icde *in* fereon pulvir.**

​	*[he] [about] [him] [often] [he-talks]*

​	He often talks about him (someone else).

### x.x. Reciprocal Pronoun

A reciprocal pronoun refers to *multiple* people doing some action to each other (*one another, each other*).

In Kotava, the reciprocal pronoun is **sint**.  It never takes a gender suffix either.  Naturally, it can only be used with plural subjects.

​	**Sin va *sint* disuked.**

​	*[they] [object >] [each-other] [they-look-at]*

​	They look at each other. (1 is looking at 2, and 2 is looking at 1.)

​	**Sin va *int* disuked.**

​	*[they] [object >] [self] [they-look-at]*

​	They each look at themselves. (1 is looking at himself, and 2 is looking at himself.)

​	**Sin va sin disuked.**

​	*[they] [object >] [them] [they-look at]*

​	They look at them. (Multiple people are looking at other people.)

### x.x. Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns, like '*mine*' or '*theirs*' are for things referred to as belonging to someone.

In Kotava, they take the form of the determiner **tel** (meaning *the* or *the one*), the personal pronoun {x.1} + adjective ending **-af**:

|               |                  |
| ------------- | ---------------- |
| **tel jinaf** | mine             |
| **tel rinaf** | yours (singular) |
| **tel inaf**  | his, hers, its   |
| **tel minaf** | ours (inclusive) |
| **tel cinaf** | ours (exclusive) |
| **tel winaf** | yours (plural)   |
| **tel sinaf** | theirs           |

​	**Tel sinaf tir lokaliaf dam jinaf.**

​	*[the-one] [they-adj.] [it-is] [fast-er] [than] [the-one] [I-adj.]*

​	His is faster than mine. 

There is also a reflexive version of this:

​	**Va tel intaf estuyur.**

​	*[object >] [the-one] [self-adj.] [he-ate]*

​	He ate his own.

And there are two other forms we will cover later ([x.x](Determiners.md)).

​	**tel dal intaf**

​	**tel dil intaf**
