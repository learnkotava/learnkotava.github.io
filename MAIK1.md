## Lesson 1. Pronunciation

#### Rules

### 1.1. Elements of Pronunciation

In Kotava words are written phonetically, that is they are written as they are pronounced (and vice versa).  All letters must be pronounced.  The alphabet has 24 letter-phonemes.

The following letters have a pronunciation that totally or somewhat differs from English pronunciation:

| Letter | Sound                                                        |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| *c*    | sh as in <u>sh</u>ort                                        |
| *e*    | ay as in d<u>ay</u>                                          |
| *g*    | always hard g as in <u>g</u>uitar                            |
| *j*    | zh, as in mea<u>s</u>ure                                     |
| *s*    | always soft s as in <u>s</u>and                              |
| *u*    | oo as in c<u>oo</u>                                          |
| *x*    | kh, or a Spanish j as in <u>J</u>ose                         |
| *y*    | always as in <u>y</u>es, never a vowel sound like in cr<u>y</u>stal or m<u>y</u>riad |

The letter *r* is generally rolled, like in Italian, Spanish or Russian.  That said, the type of *r* sound used is up to you.

The other letters of Kotava: *a, b, d, f, i, k, l, m, n, o, p, t, v* are pronounced lik in English.  There is no *h* or *q* in Kotava.

Kotava has 5 vowels: *a, e, i, o, u*.  All vowels must be pronounced separately and a word has as many syllables as it has vowels.

| Kotava Word   | Meaning | Pronunciation in English |
| ------------- | ------- | ------------------------ |
| **mona**      | house   | [MOH-nah]                |
| **vegedú**    | I build | [veh-geh-DOO]            |
| **batcoba**   | this    | [baht-SHO-bah]           |
| **anyustoy**  | eight   | [ah-nyoo-STOY]           |
| **xefto**     | storm   | [KHEF-toh]               |
| **widava**    | city    | [wee-DAH-vah]            |
| **danká**     | I sing  | [dahn-KAH]               |
| **tawadayaf** | rural   | [tah-wah-dah-YAHF]       |

> ***Note:*** In Kotava, the stress always lands on the last closed syllable, that is the last syllable when the word ends in a consonant and the next-to-last syllable when it ends in a vowel.
> 
> That said, there's one exception, which comes in for verbs conjugated in the first person singular.  In that case, the stress lands on the last syllable.  This characteristic final vowel thus gets an acute accent mark to signal this stress: *-á, -é, -í, ú*.
