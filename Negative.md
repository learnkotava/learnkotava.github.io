## x. Negative

### x.1. me - 'not'

In Kotava, the word for '*not*' is **me**.  It is placed right before the verb you are negating.

​	**Me estú**.

​	*[not] [I-eat]*

​	I am not eating.

​	**Koe Paris me irubá.**

​	*[in] [Paris] [not] [I-live]*

​	I do not live in Paris.

​	**Va in me kiaval.**

​	*[object >] [he] [not] [you-greet]*

​	You are not greeting him.

### x.2. men - 'not yet'

To say "not yet," use **men**, also right before the verb, in the same place as **me**:

​	**Koe Paris men irubá.**

​	*[in] [Paris] [not-yet] [I-live]*

​	I do not live in Paris yet.

​	**Sin va sint men kiavad.**

​	*[they] [object >] [each-other] [not-yet] [they-greet]*

​	They are not greeting each other yet. / They are not yet greeting each other.

### x.3. mea - 'not anymore'

To say "not anymore," use **mea**, again right before the verb:

​	**Koe Paris mea irubá.**

​	*[in] [Paris] [not-anymore] [I live]*

​	I don't live in Paris anymore.

​	**Sin va sint mea kiavad.**

​	*[they] [object >] [each-other] [not-anymore] [they-greet]*

​	They're not greeting each other anymore.

​	**Noelon, viele va int tuidulá, bam kan Kotava mea otá kire va Solresol ava noelton toz raveyé.**

​	*[currently] [when] [object >] [oneself] [I-heat-up], [then] [by-means-of] [Kotava] [not-anymore] [count] [because] [object >] [Solresol] [language] [recently] [start-to] [I-learned].

​	*(context: counting to ten during exercise)* Currently, when I warm up, I no longer rely on the Kotava language because I recently started learning the Solresol language.  ([Tutuzara](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p2C5lZOUL1k&t=63) ~ 2018)

### x.4. vol - 'not at all'

Kotava also has a useful word, **vol**, for the idea that not only does the verb not happen, *all is done for it not to happen.*  It is best translated as "not at all," but Kotava materials also translate it as "on the contrary," "in contrast to," or "surely not".  Look at these examples, and you'll get the idea.

​	**Vol estú.**

​	*[contrary] [I-eat]*

​	I <u>do my best not to</u> eat.

​	**Sin va sint vol kiavad.**

​	*[they] [object >] [each-other] [contrary] [they-greet]*

​	They <u>do all it takes to not</u> greet each other.

​	**Xum, dile vol!** 

​	*[hmm] [sometimes] [contrary]*

​	Hm! It's sometimes <u>the opposite</u>.  ([Va Mona Anamlaní](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EwMjcQIZbFg&t=240) ~ 2017)

​	**Paelpa toz aultover neken vol idular.  Adim vol idulayar vomion.**

​	*[sweating] [start-to] [it-abounds] [nonetheless] [contrary] [it-is-hot].  [finally] [contrary] [it-was-hot] [before]*

​	(*colloquially, context is after jumping up and down outside*): 

​	The sweating really starts, yet it's <u>the furthest thing from</u> hot out.  --At least, it <u>was the furthest thing from</u> hot out before.  

> *Original translation from video*: Sweat begins to abound, yet it is absolutely not hot. Finally,<mark>*</mark> it was absolutely not hot before.  ([Utiura](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YH-epGXpWyo&t=58s) ~ 2018)
> 
> * *<mark>Note</mark>: - The word **adim** can mean "finally," as Luce has translated it for the video captions, but would in this context be a slight error due to the French translation for **adim**, which is "enfin."  The meaning of **adim** here is closer to "after all," or "oh well" but a French speaker's use of "enfin" here is more like the concept behind "at least" as we have it here.* To understand this correction, you can read more about [enfin](https://www.thoughtco.com/enfin-vocabulary-1371954).

**Ombamukot tir rodjesira va kevelik, i debala tode gazikye zo rogralomer ise vol rototcer.**

*[game+objective] [it-is] [cause-the-failure] [object>] [adversary], [< substitute] [situation] [in-which-situation] [king-male] [passive >] [it-can-capture] [and] [contrary] [it-can-escape]*

*(context: chess)* objective of the game is to [checkmate] your opponent, that being the situation where the king is able to get captured and <u>has no way to escape</u>.
