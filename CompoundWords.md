## x. Compound Words

Compound words in English are made of multiple words combined, such as seahorse, foxglove.  But in English, these words are somewhat fixed -- instead of making *new* compound words, you have to use multiple separate words: like coffee mug, picture frame, or court case.

In Kotava, you can be a lot more creative with forming compound words.  There are 4 main ways to do it.

---

### x.1. Noun + Noun ➡️ Compound Noun

You can combine two nouns together, if they have one of the following relationships:

* object / transitive (*va*, i.e. one is the object of the other)
  
  **iaxe** (factory)
  **tie** (gas)
  **iaxe *va* tie** (gas-fired power plant)
  *[manufacture-location] [object >] [gas]*
  	➡️ **tieiaxe** (gas-fired power plant)
* possession / ownership (*ke*, i.e. one possesses the other)
  
  **widava *ke* winka** (provincial town)
  *[town] [of] [province]*
  	➡️ **winkawidava** (provincial town)
* content / partitive (*dem*, i.e. one contains the other)
  
  galema *dem* lava (glass of water)
  *[glass] [containing] [water]*
  	➡️ **lavagalema** (glass of water)
  
* destination (*tori*, i.e. one is used for another, its "destined use" is the other, think "for", like a vase for flowers or a cup for coffee)
  
  **bilaga *tori* fadxa** (cup for coffee)
  *[cup] [used-for] [coffee]*
  	➡️ **fadxabilaga** (coffee cup)
* purpose (*ta*, i.e. [])
  
  **bruxa *ta* sutera**
  *[feather] [goal >] [writing]*
  	➡️ **suterabruxa** (quill)
  
* matter (*kum*, i.e. one is made out of the other, one is the material of the other)
  
  **kudja *kum* inta**
  *[statue] [made-of] [wood]*
  	➡️ **intakudja **(wood statue)


Luckily, if you know the two nouns have one of these relationships, but can't precisely figure out which one, it is often easier to compound them anyway rather than trying to pinpoint the right preposition.

---

### x.2. Adjective + Noun ➡️ Compound Noun

You can add a noun and its adjective together, too, but only one attribute can be combined with its noun (not multiple).

Here's how:

1. Strip down adjective to stem by removing adjective ending: 
   *pulodafa ➡️  pulod-, bemafa ➡️ pemaf-*.

   * *Note:* With verbal adjectives {}, you won't strip it down first.

2. Add an **-a** if the noun starts in a consonant.

3. Add the result to the front of the noun:

   **pulod** (parliament)
   **vertuma** (system, regime)
   pulodafa vertuma
   *[parliament (adj.)] [system]*
   *➡️ pulod~~afa~~ vertuma*
   *➡️ pulod + **a** + vertuma*
   	➡️ **pulodavertuma** 'parliamentary system'

   **bema** (school)
   **neva** (book)
   bemafa neva
   *[school (adj.)] [book]*
   *➡️ bem~~afa~~ neva*
   *➡️ bem+ **a** + neva*
   	➡️ **bemaneva** 'schoolbook'

4. Only one adjective can be compounded this way:

   **warzafa** (new)

   ❌ ~~**warzabemaneva**~~
   ✅ **warzafa bemaneva**
   ✅ **warzafa bemafa neva**

> *Note:* Compounding like this is always optional, i.e. 'pulodafa vertuma' and 'bemafa neva' are allowed and mean the same thing.

### x.3. Determiner + Substantive (Time) ➡️ Adverb (Time)

Compound words are often to make adverbs of time that express a phrase about time using prepositions like **ba** (at, in), **bak** (during), or **remi** (while), e.g.:

​	**ba kot bartiv** (every hour)

Instead of the preposition, you compound the determiner and the time noun (removing euphonic endings {[x.x](Nouns.md)}, and removing -**a** from time nouns ending in **-a**), and add the adverb ending **-on** {}:

​	**ba kot bartiv** (every hour)
​	*➡️ ~~ba~~ kot + bartiv + **on***
​		➡️ **kotbartivon** (every hour (adv.))

​	**ba kota gazda** (every morning)
​	*➡️ ~~ba~~ kot~~a~~ + gazd~~a~~ + **on***
​		➡️ **kotgazdon** (every morning (adv.))

​	**bak bete sare** (from time immemorial, in any age)
​	*➡️ ~~bak~~ bet~~e~~ + sare + **on***
​		➡️ **betsareon** (from time immemorial (adv.))

​	**ba bata vula** (in this instant)
​	*➡️ ~~ba~~ bat~~a~~ + vula + **on***
​		➡️ **batvulon** (this instant (adv.))

​	**ba mek gemelt** (in no moment, at no point in time)
​	*➡️ ~~ba~~ mek + gemelt + **on***
​		➡️ **mekgemelton** (at no point in time (adv.))

Example:

> "***<u>Kotvielon is kotbartivon</u>*, kota gasiza is kot awalkik va bata tiguafa zobexa loloon sovibad ise tanda kalion rucad.**"
> "<u>*Every day and every hour*</u> every shell and every death cuts into this thin support, and the years waste it rapidly." Erich Maria Remarque, *All Quiet on the Western Front*.

You can also combine this way for prepositional phrases using **mali** (since) and **kali** (until):

​	**mali arintaf viel** (since yesterday)
​	*➡️ mali + arint~~af~~ + **on***
​		➡️ **maliarinton** (since yesterday (adv.))

​	**kali eldef viel** (until tomorrow)
​	*➡️ kali + eld~~ef~~ + **on***
​		➡️ **kalieldeon** (until tomorrow (adv.))

This compounding happens with nouns having to do with time, mainly:

​	**afiz** (daytime), **aksat** (month), **bartiv** (hour), **gazda** (early morning), **gemelt** (moment), **kiel** (afternoon), **miafiz** (noon), **riel** (morning), **safta** (week), **sare** (age), **siel** (evening), **tanda** (year), **ugal** (time), **vanafiz** (dawn), **verast** (second), **viel** (day), **vula** (instant), **wexa** (minute).

It also applies to the **-cek** {x.x} forms of these nouns (duration or content), such as:

​	**afizcek** (daytime), **gazdacek** (morning period), **kielcek** (afternoon period), **rielcek** (morning period), **sielcek** (evening period), **vielcek** (whole day).

### x.4. 'One-by-One' ➡️ Adverb

You can make an adverb to express progression, repetition, or iteration, in the sense of 'piece by piece' or 'one by one', by compounding the term with itself (removing **-a** from the first term) and again adding adverb ending -**on**:

​	**bar** (3)
​	*➡️ bar + bar + **on**:*
​		➡️ **barbaron** (*in threes, 3-by-3*)

​	**abic** (a little)
​	*➡️ abic + abic + **on**:*
​		➡️ **abicabicon** (*little by little*)

​	**tuvel** (door)
​	*➡️ tuvel + tuvel + **on**:*
​		➡️ **tuveltuvelon** (*door to door*)

Another example, from the Kotava official grammar:

​	**kalikalion** (*making great strides*)
