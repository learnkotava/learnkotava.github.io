You can do *a lot* in Kotava with these types of pronouns.  Luckily, it's all in a very logical system.  Take your time!

### x.1. Relative Pronouns 🚧

A relative pronoun, like 'who,' 'which', or 'that', lets you describe something using a verb nested within the larger sentence.  For example, 'I like dogs <u>*that* know tricks</u>' or 'This is the man <u>*who* called yesterday</u>'.

> <u>Note:</u> Kotava doesn't use these the way English does, instead opting for participles ([x.x](Participles.md)) -- in short, instead of "the person who eats bread" the Kotava sentence looks more like "the person eating bread."

There are four basic compound relative pronouns:

​	**coba** (*thing*, as a relative pronoun meaning *what, that which*)

​	**tan** (*one*, as a relative pronoun meaning *one (unknown)*)

​	**tel** (*the*, as a relative pronoun meaning *one (known)*, *the one, this one*)

​	**tol** (*two*, as a relative pronoun meaning *either*) 

### x.x.1. Pronoun Building

Kotava has a fun, easy-to-learn system for making these kinds of pronouns.  They are made out of pieces that become familiar quickly.  We will go through each of the pieces and their meanings when combined with the 4 basic relative pronouns above.

The types of pronouns here include: demonstratives (like 'this' or 'that'), negative (like 'nobody' or 'nowhere'), indefinite (like 'somewhere', 'anybody', 'none', 'everyone' or 'most'), quantitative (like 'few', 'many', 'several ones' or 'too many') and interrogative (like 'which one' or 'which kind').

Since the pieces do not change in strange ways like other languages, building new ways to express what you want in Kotava can feel more like a game than a chore.

Each of the pieces below are also Kotava words in their own right, like in English, e.g. 'anybody' and 'somebody', and 'nobody' are all made of pieces added to '-body' that are words on their own (any, some, no).

### x.x.2. bat- 

**Bat** means *this*.

On its own, it takes euphony:

​	**bata gola**

​	*this region*

​	**bate blucte**

​	*these clothes*

|         |                           |
| ------- | ------------------------- |
| batcoba | this (this thing)         |
| battan  | this (this one) (unknown) |
| battel  | this (this one) (known)   |
| battol  | this one (of the two)     |

**Bak IX-eafa decemda Kilwa Kisiwani ewala gan Ali bin Al-Hasan dolekik zo luster. Battan ton kazas molt di voner.**

During the 9th century the isle of Kilwa Kisiwani was bought by the merchant Ali bin Al-Hasan.  This (unknown) [would go on to] develop as a trading bridge.

**Joweem ke suteroteem tire tir klubaf ise sedme kottan miv gunestes arapeniwer.**

Literature's set of boundaries is obviously vague, and is varied according to everyone evaluating on their own.

**Batkase lantan va pemaxo geltron kosmad.**

In this case, certain people really discover such an encampment. 

**Lantan va in wetce afievik pulad.**

There are those who / Some classify him as a luminist.

🚧

**lantel** = a certain someone
